From drawing to installation, in six steps

Every Calibro project follows the same six steps: drawings review, shop drawings, samples and prototypes, production, quality control and installation. Each step ends in a sign-off, and nothing is made or changed without the designer's written approval. The drawing is the contract.

The six steps

  1. 01

    Drawings review

    Sign-off: the brief, the scope and the open questions, agreed in writing.

    We read your drawings, specification and BOQ as a maker reads them: joints, tolerances, finishes, access and sequence. Questions are raised at this stage, before they turn into variations on site. The scope is confirmed piece by piece, and the quotation is itemised against your schedule.

  2. 02

    Shop drawings

    Sign-off: approved shop drawings for every piece.

    Each piece is detailed for production: construction, substrates, hardware, fixings, wiring and the finish schedule, with every dimension checked against the site survey. The drawings come back to you for approval. Nothing is cut before they are signed.

  3. 03

    Samples and prototypes

    Sign-off: approved finish samples and, for critical pieces, an approved prototype.

    Veneers, stains, lacquers, stone, metals, fabrics and leathers are sampled in the specified references, or in a matched alternative proposed for your approval. Critical pieces get a first-off or prototype, reviewed against the shop drawing before the order is released to production.

  4. 04

    Production

    Sign-off: in-production hold-point inspections by Calibro.

    Production is placed with an audited UAE partner workshop chosen for the materials and the scale of the package, and runs under Calibro's supervision to the approved drawings and samples. Calibro does not own a factory; it owns the drawings, the programme and the inspections.

  5. 05

    Quality control

    Sign-off: the pre-delivery release, scored against the approved drawing.

    Before anything leaves the workshop it is checked against the approved shop drawing and samples: dimensions, construction, finish, hardware and function. Each piece is scored out of 100 and released at 90 or above; anything below is corrected and inspected again.

  6. 06

    Installation and handover

    Sign-off: the snag list closed, the handover documents issued.

    Pieces are delivered to site, installed and levelled by Calibro's team, coordinated with the contractor's programme. The snag list is walked with you and closed before handover, and aftercare follows once the space is in use.

Questions about the process

How long does a bespoke furniture project take?

The lead time depends on the size of the package, the materials and the approvals. The programme is agreed at order, with the hold points marked, and tracked against the approvals as the project runs. Ask for a programme with your quotation.

Can the design change after the order?

Yes, before the relevant hold point. A change is priced and programmed, and made only once the designer has approved it in writing. Nothing is substituted without that approval.

What is a hold point?

A stage at which production stops until an inspection is passed or a sign-off is given: approved shop drawings, approved samples, a first-off or prototype, in-production checks, the pre-delivery release and the installation snag list.

Who installs the furniture?

Calibro's installation team, coordinated with the main contractor or the client's site team, with a snag list closed at handover.
