Value engineering that keeps the design intent

Value engineering at Calibro changes what does not show: substrates, construction methods, hardware, material yield and logistics. The visible design, the finishes and the proportions stay as drawn, and every proposed change is submitted to the designer for written approval before it is made.

Where the cost is engineered, and where it never is

The same drawing, built for less. Not a different drawing.

Where it is

  • Substrates: the board under a veneer or lacquer, chosen for the duty rather than by habit
  • Construction: frames, joints and internal structure that reach the same strength with less material or labour
  • Hardware: hinges, runners, mechanisms and fixings of equal performance from a different maker
  • Yield: how veneers, fabrics, leathers and stone are cut from the sheet, hide or slab
  • Logistics: knock-down versus assembled delivery, packing, sequencing and the number of site visits

Where it never is

  • The visible finish: the veneer species, the stone, the fabric, the leather, the colour
  • The proportions and dimensions the designer drew
  • The details the client sees and touches: edges, reveals, stitching, hardware faces
  • Performance the project requires: fire ratings, load, durability, warranty
  • Anything not approved in writing by the designer
( 02 )

The value-engineering register

Every proposal written down, with its saving and its effect.
  1. 01

    Read

    At the drawings review, each piece is read for cost drivers: material, construction, hardware, finish and logistics.

  2. 02

    Propose

    Each proposed change is listed with the drawing reference, the saving, the effect on programme and what, if anything, changes visibly. Usually nothing.

  3. 03

    Approve

    The designer and client accept or decline line by line. Declined lines stay in the register as a record.

  4. 04

    Build

    Accepted changes go into the shop drawings and samples, and are inspected at the same hold points as everything else.

Questions about value engineering

What is value engineering in furniture and FF&E?

Finding a way to build the same design for less, or faster, by changing how a piece is made rather than how it looks: the substrate under a veneer, the construction of a frame, the hardware, how material is cut from a sheet, how pieces are packed and delivered.

Will value engineering change the look of the design?

Not at Calibro. The visible design, the finishes and the proportions stay as drawn. Changes are confined to what the client cannot see or touch, and each one is submitted to the designer for written approval before it is made.

When should value engineering happen?

At the drawings review, before the quotation is fixed. A value-engineering register issued with the quotation lists each proposed change, its saving and its effect, so the designer and client decide with the numbers in front of them.
