Working with Calibro: a guide for interior designers and architects

You draw it; Calibro builds it as drawn. This page sets out what to send, what comes back, how shop drawings and samples are approved, how value engineering is kept under your control, and how your design is credited. It applies to a single feature piece and to a complete FF&E package.

Send your drawings
Your drawing
Is the contract
Approvals
Shop drawings, samples, prototypes, changes: yours, in writing
Value engineering
Proposed in a register, never assumed
Credit
The design is yours; Calibro is credited for production

What to send us

  • Plans, elevations and sections of each piece, or a dimensioned reference image; DWG, PDF or both
  • A finishes schedule or specification with references: timber, veneer, stone, metal, fabric, leather
  • Quantities, the programme and the site
  • A BOQ, if one exists; pricing follows your schedule line by line
  • Anything already decided with the client: budget range, fixed items, priorities

What comes back

  • Questions first, raised before they become variations on site
  • An itemised quotation against your schedule, with a value-engineering register if there are proposals
  • A programme with the hold points where you sign off
  • Shop drawings for every piece, for your approval before production
  • Physical samples of each finish and a prototype of each critical piece
( 01 )

Approvals and changes

Nothing is cut before the shop drawing is signed. Nothing is substituted without written approval.
Shop drawings
Issued piece by piece for your approval. Comments are incorporated and reissued until signed.
Samples and prototypes
Finishes sampled in your references; critical pieces prototyped and reviewed with you before the order runs.
Changes
Priced and programmed before they are made, and made only after your written approval, up to the relevant hold point.
Value engineering
A register of proposals with savings and effects; you accept or decline each line. How it works.

Confidentiality and credit

Your drawings and specifications are shared only with the partner workshop producing the piece, under confidentiality. A project is published only with the client's and your consent, and the design is credited to you: on this site every project names its designer, and Calibro is credited for engineering and production.

Photographs of completed work are shared with you for your own portfolio where the client agrees.

Site and installation

Calibro surveys the site for fixed joinery, confirms readiness before delivery, installs with its own team to the contractor's programme and walks the snag list with you before handover. Aftercare follows once the interior is occupied. Installation and aftercare.

Questions designers ask

Will Calibro change my design to make it cheaper to build?

No. Value engineering at Calibro changes what does not show, and every proposed change is submitted to you in a register for written approval before it is made. Declined proposals are not built.

Who owns the shop drawings?

The design stays yours. Calibro's shop drawings are produced for your project and submitted for your approval.

Are my drawings kept confidential?

Yes. Drawings and specifications are shared only with the partner workshop producing the piece, under confidentiality, and Calibro publishes a project only with the client's and designer's consent, with the design credited to you.

Can I visit the workshop during production?

Yes, by arrangement, for prototype reviews and in-production inspections. Calibro accompanies every visit.
